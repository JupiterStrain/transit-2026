WHAT IS IN THE FRAME

Both reported components are present, in the same data, and they are not
the same object.

    1.  TWO CURVED RIMS entering the field from opposite margins and
        converging. Measured width 0.68", deconvolved 0.40" +/- 0.04".
        Ridge surface brightness 32.8 +/- 3.6 mJy/arcsec^2 at its
        brightest, detected at 14 sigma per beam. It is not uniform: the
        ridge varies by about a third along each arc, with structure on
        scales of one to three arcseconds, and the pattern is different
        on the two rims.

    2.  AN UNRESOLVED SOURCE in the gap between them, on the axis of
        convergence, FWHM 0.552" and consistent with a point at every
        radius we fit. Flux 30.1 +/- 2.4 mJy.

    separation of the rims at closest approach   2.04" +/- 0.05"
    circle fitted to each rim, radius            9.0"  +/- 0.4"
    arc of each rim crossing this field          14.2"
    surface inside this field                    9.36 arcsec^2

WHAT THIS FRAME DOES AND DOES NOT MEASURE. It is one filter. A single
photometric point cannot fit a temperature, and we have not fitted one.
The two temperatures quoted below were measured elsewhere — 1150 K from
the CVIS near-infrared colours, 50 K from the Hyperion 70/160 ratio — and
this frame is consistent with both and confirms neither. What it measures
is a geometry, and the geometry is the result.

WHY THE RIMS ARE RIMS. The brightness runs along the arcs and stops
sharply on both sides of each. That is what a thin surface looks like
when the sight line grazes it: bright where we look along the surface,
dark where we look through it. It is a geometric effect and not an
optical depth effect, and it is why we see two lines rather than two
discs. Against the 51.9 K the far-infrared implies at this epoch, the
ridge at its brightest is 1.00 +/- 0.11 of a blackbody, and a surface
cannot exceed one. So the variation along the ridge is not temperature.
It is how completely the sight line is filled, and at the brightest
points it is filled entirely.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHY THE TWO EARLIER REPORTS BOTH STAND

    at 2.15 um   the cold rims contribute 9e-45 mJy inside this field.
                 CVIS sees the hot source and nothing else, and reports
                 it unresolved, which it is.

    at 18.0 um   rims 307 mJy inside this field, point source 30 mJy.
                 Both measurable in the same data, and separated on the
                 sky by more than the beam. This is the only window in
                 which that is true.

    at 70 um     the 1150 K source contributes 2.6 mJy against 1,888 Jy.
                 Hyperion sees the rims and nothing else, and its 20.7"
                 beam cannot separate them, so it reports one extended
                 object, which is a fair account of what reaches it.

Neither observatory was in error. Neither was looking at the whole thing.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------

SCALE

Geocentric distance on 11 April is 24.90 AU, at which 1" subtends
18,056 km.

    rim separation          2.04"     =   36,800 km
    rim width (deconvolved) 0.40"     =    7,230 km
    radius of curvature     9.0"      =  162,500 km
    this frame             12.75"     =  230,000 km

MEASURED, AND EXTRAPOLATED. Everything above is inside the detector.
Everything below is a circle fit run off the edge of it, and should be
read as such by anybody using these numbers downstream:

    one structure across   18.0"      =  325,000 km   (2.32 Jupiters)
    the pair across        38.0"      =  687,000 km
    total curved surface   35.7 arcsec^2

Only 9.36 of those 35.7 arcsec^2 were imaged. The rest is what two arcs
of 14.2" extrapolate to if they are circles, and 35" of extended emission
in the Hyperion map is the only independent support it has. Anyone
carrying the 35.7 forward into an area, a mass or an albedo is carrying
a circle fit, and should say so.

THE SOURCE IN THE GAP. Its temperature and flux give an emitting area of
6.16e07 m^2, which is an area and not a shape. If it is circular it is
8.9 km across. All this image can say is that it is smaller than 0.06",
or 1,000 km, which is where our PSF fit stops distinguishing it from a
point. Every statement in this archive about the size of the hot
component is that limit and not a measurement.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT WE ARE NOT SAYING

We are reporting a geometry. We are not proposing a mechanism, and we
would ask that the frame not be circulated with one attached.

We are aware of how it looks. Two large surfaces almost in contact, with
an 1150 K source in the gap, is the arrangement you would draw if you
were asked to draw friction. We note that the gap is 36,800 km wide and
that nothing is touching anything, that nothing in the outer solar system
is known to do any part of this, that the energy budget is discussed in
the astrometric solution circulated separately, and that the caption we
have put on the public release ends in a question mark for a reason.
